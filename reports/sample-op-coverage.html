02Every problem, by nameFailures

A failure should have
a name.

A score tells you how far you are. A name tells you what to do on Monday.

In this run, nothing failed. Two ops weren’t supported: integer divide and modulo. Inferentia2’s compiler refuses them, and the report quotes the backend word for word.

What FuseZap does

What it means here.

The models we serve today don’t need them. In Llama, Qwen and Mistral, the RoPE tables and the embedding lookup run on the host. That’s why all three families stay reachable on a chip without integer divide.

03On the real chip26 Oct 2026

Three tests
out of 1,174.

A clean sample doesn’t show you what a failure looks like. Our first full run on a real Inferentia2 does. On 26 October, 1,171 of 1,174 operation tests passed on the chip. Three didn’t.

That run came before fz-opcheck. Same tests, run straight from the suite, so it reads as raw test output.

What FuseZap does

What they meant.

exp2 and tanh. The chip’s built-in approximations are a little less precise than our tests allow. Not a crash. A tolerance, missed by a little. We replaced them with exact versions.

log2. It passed the suite. A wider check found it wrong for some inputs. That’s a bug, not a tolerance.

By the next day, exp, exp2 and log2 matched the simulator bit for bit, and the suite ran on the chip with no failures.

04 · The rest

What it would take for the rest.

From this run’s own gaps. Yours will be about your chip.

Not supported

Integer divide and modulo

Only needed by models that index on the device. On a chip without them, the backend would have to emulate them from ops the chip has. That isn’t built yet.

Inferred

bfloat16 cases

For each covered family, 13 of the ops it needs have no bfloat16 case in the suite, so the report reads their float32 or int32 result instead, and says so. Thin bfloat16 coverage is a known limit of the prototype.

Not covered yet

Other model families

Mixture of experts, Gemma, Phi, DeepSeek-V3, vision-language models and Mamba aren’t checked yet. The models table above says what each one adds.

The report doesn’t check the serving engine’s paged-KV kernels or on-device argmax; those are custom kernels in the backend. It has no speed figure: its timings are per case and include compiling. How the tool works →
